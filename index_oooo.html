About the studio

From a very wide perspective, design is not a single discipline or a profession, it is a family of professions. It includes, or historically included, urban design (the design of urban places), regional planning (concerned with the structure and ecology of whole regions), and the type of town planning that produces plans for the physical structures of cities. Architecture is often believed to be the mother of these professions, although, now, one might argue that it less of a prototypical design profession now. But, like all families, this family also has some issues: Wittgenstenian issues. Design has stopped resembling like architecture and has taken its shapes and forms. These forms might be similar to management, or software development, or fabrication engineering, consulting, and so forth. It is also blurred with engineering, technology design, creative technologies, engineering design, design engineering, and management. And this blurring, merging is good; it indicates evolution of design as a profession. With these changes, a designer needs to speak like a designer but also speak to machines, and technologies. A Designer (a capital D!) needs to have technological imagination.

This course is about seeing designer in relation to technology. It aims to expand on the technological imaginations through observing, thinking, and making -- all three in a particular rhythm. It aims to create tangible, embedded, embodied, interactive, and reactive products with consciousness of technology, of designer's roles in shaping technology, and its effects and affect on the user.
