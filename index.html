Why technology and design situations

Problems do not arrive as "a UX task" or "an electronics task". They arrive as a bus stop with no timetable, or a shop ledger nobody trusts. This course starts there, and picks up methods and tools as the situation asks for them.

Four reasonsWhy not projects or tools
Tangled

Real problems cross disciplines

A broken queue involves layout, signage, payment, staffing and mood at once. A situation keeps all of them in view. A single-discipline brief hides most of them.

Chosen

Technology is a decision

Every system has someone who decided what it measures, hides or automates. Inside a situation you can see who decided, and who lives with the result.

Bounded

Limits teach faster than briefs

Budget, power cuts, language and time are part of the situation. You learn to design inside them instead of for an ideal user in an ideal lab.

Durable

Tools expire, judgement carries

Software and frameworks change every few years. Observing, framing, testing and arguing for a choice moves with you to the next tool.

Anatomy of a situationWorked example: the hostel mess queue
Field
It asks
Illustrative example
Who
Who is in it, and who is missing?
Students after a late class, mess workers, the supervisor. A student with a food allergy is easy to overlook.
Where
What is the physical and social setting?
A hall with two counters, one door and a 20-minute window before the rush.
What breaks
What goes wrong, observed rather than assumed?
The line folds back on itself near the tray stack. Some people leave without eating.
At stake
What does failure cost, and to whom?
Missed meals, wasted food, staff blamed for a layout they did not choose.
On hand
Which technology, materials and skills are already there?
A tablet at the counter, a shared spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group, two stopwatches.
